# Concept network — strategy and the middlegame

# imbalances | strategy | الاختلالات (أساس الخطة) | Imbalances
refs: silman-reassess, silman-amateurs-mind, silman-workbook, seirawan-play-winning, euwe-judgement, silman-complete-strategy
rel: plan-making, bishop-vs-knight, space, weak-squares, initiative
pz: c_middlegame
> Every position contains differences between the two sides — minor pieces, pawn structure, space, material, control of files and squares, development, initiative, king safety. A good plan uses the imbalances that favour you and neutralises those that favour the opponent.
+ List the imbalances before choosing a move; the plan follows from them, not from the threats alone.
+ Static imbalances (structure, bishop vs knight) last; dynamic ones (development, initiative) must be used quickly or they disappear.
= Ask three questions: what is different? which differences favour me? where on the board should I play?
! Playing "natural" moves without a plan, or reacting only to the opponent's threats.

# plan-making | strategy | وضع الخطة | Making a plan
refs: euwe-judgement, romanovsky-planning, kotov-play-gm, mcdonald-planning, capablanca-fundamentals, karpov-find-plan, steinitz-modern-instructor, suetin-middlegame, gelfer-handbook, giddins-50-lessons, nunn-understanding, botvinnik-selected
rel: imbalances, worst-piece, three-questions, pawn-breaks, prophylaxis
pz: c_middlegame
> A plan is a series of moves aimed at one goal derived from the position: attacking a weakness, using an open file, advancing a majority, improving a bad piece. Even a modest plan is better than aimless moves.
+ Steinitz's rule: the side with the advantage must attack, otherwise the advantage disappears; the side without it should defend and wait.
+ Plans come from the pawn structure: it tells you where to play and which pieces are good.
+ A plan must be checked concretely — calculation confirms or refutes it.
= Steps: assess (imbalances) → choose the target → find the best squares for each piece → find the pawn break → execute while watching the opponent's counterplay.
! Changing plans every move; or sticking to a plan when the position has changed.

# worst-piece | strategy | تحسين أسوأ قطعة | Improve your worst piece

rel: three-questions, knight-on-the-rim, good-bad-bishop

> When nothing concrete is happening, find your worst piece and give it a better role — often the best plan in quiet positions (Karpov was a master of this).
+ Equally important: keep the opponent's worst piece bad (restrict it).

# prophylaxis | strategy | الوقاية (منع خطة الخصم) | Prophylaxis
refs: nimzowitsch-my-system, dvoretsky-positional-play, petrosian-python, karpov-find-plan, dvoretsky-resources, dvoretsky-school-strategic
rel: restriction, three-questions, plan-making
pz: p_quiet
> Before carrying out your own plan, ask what the opponent wants and prevent it. Nimzowitsch introduced the idea; Petrosian and Karpov made it a style: the opponent runs out of useful moves.
+ Prophylactic thinking is a habit: after each opponent move, ask "what does he want to do now?" and "what would he do if it were his move again?"
+ Typical prophylactic moves: luft for the king, a rook stopping a pawn break, a knight covering an entry square.
= Find the opponent's best plan; if it is dangerous, stop it; if not, continue your own plan.
! Pursuing your attack while ignoring the opponent's faster counterattack.

# centre | strategy | المركز | The centre
refs: nimzowitsch-my-system, reti-modern-ideas, euwe-kramer-middlegame, fine-ideas-openings, pachman-strategy, watson-secrets
rel: opening-principles, hypermodern, pawn-breaks, space
pz: c_middlegame
> Control of e4, d4, e5, d5 gives the pieces more scope and makes it easier to switch play to either wing. The type of centre decides the plan.
+ Open centre: pieces dominate, development and the bishops count; attacks on the king become dangerous.
+ Closed centre (blocked pawns): play on the wings, usually where your pawn chain points.
+ Mobile centre: the side with the centre pawns wants to advance; the other side blockades or attacks them.
+ Fixed centre (e.g. one pair of pawns blocked): outposts and pawn breaks decide.
= Never attack on the wing while the centre is unstable; first close or control the centre.
! Opening the centre when your king is still there.

# hypermodern | strategy | المدرسة الحديثة الفائقة | Hypermodern ideas

pz: c_middlegame
> Réti and Nimzowitsch showed that the centre can be controlled from a distance with pieces (fianchettoed bishops, knights) and attacked later by pawns; a big pawn centre can become a target.
+ Openings born from this idea: Réti, Nimzo-Indian, King's Indian, Grünfeld, Alekhine Defence, Pirc.

# space | strategy | المساحة | Space advantage
refs: tarrasch-game-of-chess, silman-reassess, seirawan-play-winning, suba-dynamic
rel: restriction, cramped-position, centre
pz: c_middlegame
> More space (pawns further advanced) gives your pieces more squares and makes regrouping easy, while the opponent's pieces get in each other's way.
+ With more space, avoid exchanges; with less space, exchange pieces to free your position.
+ Space must be backed by pieces, or advanced pawns become targets.
! Over-extending pawns without piece support.

# cramped-position | strategy | الوضع الضيق | The cramped position
refs: tarrasch-game-of-chess, suba-dynamic, euwe-kramer-middlegame
rel: space, exchanges, pawn-breaks
pz: p_defense
> "A cramped position carries the germ of defeat" (Tarrasch's idea): the defending side must free itself by exchanges and a timely pawn break — or, in modern systems like the Hedgehog, stay compact and wait for the right moment to strike.
= Free yourself with exchanges and the key pawn break (...c5, ...e5, ...d5, ...f5 depending on the structure).

# pawn-breaks | strategy | الكسر البيدقي (الرافعة) | Pawn breaks (levers)
refs: kmoch-pawn-power, shankland-small-steps, marovic-dynamic, flores-structures, soltis-pawn-structure
rel: pawn-chain, centre, plan-making
pz: c_middlegame
> A pawn move that attacks an enemy pawn to open lines or change the structure; most middlegame plans revolve around one or two possible breaks.
+ Find the break that opens lines for your pieces and prepare it with pieces first.
+ Prevent the opponent's freeing break (prophylaxis).
= Typical breaks: ...c5 and ...f6 in the French, ...e5/...c5 in the QGD, ...f5 in the King's Indian, b4–b5 in the minority attack, f4–f5 in many e4 openings.

# pawn-chain | strategy | سلسلة البيادق | Pawn chains
refs: nimzowitsch-my-system, watson-play-french, soltis-pawn-structure, flores-structures
rel: pawn-breaks, french-ideas, kid-ideas
pz: c_middlegame
> Diagonal lines of pawns (e.g. White d4–e5, Black d5–e6). Nimzowitsch: attack the chain at its base; the side whose chain points to a wing usually attacks there.
+ In the French Advance, Black attacks the base d4 with ...c5, ...Nc6, ...Qb6; White defends it and attacks on the kingside.
+ In the King's Indian, Black's chain (d6–e5) points to the kingside: ...f5 attacks White's chain at e4; White's chain (d5–e4) points to the queenside: c4–c5.

# open-file | strategy | العمود المفتوح | Open files
refs: nimzowitsch-my-system, pachman-strategy, stean-simple-chess, chernev-instructive
rel: seventh-rank, rook-endgame-principles
pz: c_middlegame
> A file without pawns; rooks use it to reach the 7th and 8th ranks. The goal of play on an open file is the entry square.
+ Occupy the file, then double rooks; prevent the enemy rooks from contesting it.
+ A half-open file (only enemy pawn on it) aims at that pawn (e.g. the c-file in many Sicilians).
= If the opponent contests the file, exchange on your terms or keep control with a doubled rook.

# seventh-rank | strategy | السطر السابع | The seventh rank

rel: open-file, active-rook
pz: p_rookEnd
> A rook on the 7th rank attacks the enemy pawns from the side and confines the king to the 8th rank; two rooks there ("pigs on the seventh") often give perpetual check or mate.
+ Often worth a pawn: in rook endings an active rook on the 7th can outweigh material.

# weak-squares | strategy | المربعات الضعيفة (الثغرات) | Weak squares and holes
refs: capablanca-fundamentals, stean-simple-chess, silman-reassess, pachman-strategy, hellsten-strategy
rel: outpost, good-bad-bishop, weak-pawns
pz: c_middlegame
> A square that can no longer be defended by a pawn ("hole"). It becomes a permanent home for an enemy piece — especially a knight.
+ Weak squares appear after pawn advances; every pawn move leaves squares behind it.
+ Weak squares of one colour (after a fianchetto bishop is exchanged, for example) can be fatal near the king.
= Identify the holes, exchange the piece that could defend them, then occupy them.

# outpost | strategy | المخفر (مربع قاعدة للحصان) | Outposts
refs: stean-simple-chess, silman-reassess, nimzowitsch-my-system, chernev-logical, hellsten-strategy
rel: weak-squares, bishop-vs-knight, knight-on-the-rim
pz: c_middlegame
> A square in the opponent's half protected by your pawn and not attackable by enemy pawns. A knight on a central outpost (d5, e5, d6…) can be stronger than a rook.
+ The best outposts are central and close to the enemy position.
= Prepare the outpost (trade the defending piece), bring the knight there, and support it with pieces.

# good-bad-bishop | strategy | الفيل الجيد والفيل السيئ | Good and bad bishops

# bishop-pair | strategy | زوج الفيلة | The bishop pair

# hanging-pawns | strategy | البيادق المعلقة | Hanging pawns
refs: baburin-iqp, flores-structures, pachman-strategy
rel: isolated-pawn, pawn-breaks
pz: c_middlegame
> Two pawns side by side on the c- and d-files (or d/e) with no neighbouring pawns. They control many central squares and are strong while they can advance, but become targets once one of them is forced forward.
+ Owner: keep them side by side, use the open b- and e-files, advance one when it opens lines.
+ Opponent: pressure them with pieces (rooks on c/d files, Qa4/Qa3) to force one to advance and leave holes.
