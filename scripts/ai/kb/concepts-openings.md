# Concept network — openings and the coaches' styles

# opening-principles | opening | مبادئ الافتتاح | Opening principles
refs: lasker-common-sense, capablanca-fundamentals, fine-ideas-openings, seirawan-play-winning, mednis-good-opening-moves, hellsten-opening-strategy, tamburro-amateurs, kasparov-teaches, lasker-primer, nunn-learn-chess, weeramantry-best-lessons, euwe-meiden-amateur, mcdonald-logical
rel: centre, development-lead, castling, early-queen, opening-traps
pz: opening
> Fight for the centre with pawns and pieces, develop the minor pieces quickly (usually knights before bishops), castle early, connect the rooks, and do not waste time — each move should add something.
+ Don't move the same piece twice and don't bring the queen out early without a concrete reason.
+ Don't grab pawns at the cost of development, especially pawns near your own king's side.
+ Modern view (Mednis, Watson): the rules are guidelines; a concrete reason (winning material, stopping castling) can justify breaking them.
= A quick checklist after the opponent's move: did he create a threat? Can I develop with tempo? Is my king safe enough to open the centre?
! Moving many pawns early, an early queen sortie, and leaving the king in the centre when lines open.

# castling | opening | التبييت | Castling and king safety
refs: lasker-common-sense, chernev-logical, seirawan-play-winning
rel: opening-principles, king-in-centre, attack-castled-king
pz: castling
> Castling brings the king to safety and the rook towards the centre. Castle early when the centre may open; delay it when the centre is closed or when you don't yet know which side is safer.

# fianchetto | opening | التطوير الجانبي (الفيانكيتو) | The fianchetto
refs: reti-modern-ideas, watson-secrets, avrukh-catalan, bologan-kid, rowson-grunfeld
rel: hypermodern, weak-squares
pz: opening
> Developing the bishop on g2/b2 (g7/b7) to work on the long diagonal. It controls the centre from afar and protects the king, but if the bishop is exchanged, the squares around the king (f3/h3 or f6/h6) become weak.

# london-ideas | opening | أفكار نظام لندن | London System ideas
refs: johnsen-london, sielecki-kis-d4, fco-sterren
rel: pawn-chain, stonewall, opening-principles
pz: opening
> White sets up d4, Bf4, e3, c3, Nf3, Nd2/Nbd2, Bd3 and castles: a solid triangle (c3–d4–e3) with the bishop outside the chain. Plans: Ne5 with f4 (a Stonewall-like attack), Qc2/Bd3 battery against h7, or e3–e4 in the centre.
+ Against ...c5 keep c3 and answer ...Qb6 with Qb3 or Qc1; against ...Bd6 the bishop retreats to g3 to keep the long diagonal.
line: d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3 c5 c3 Nc6 Nd2 e6 Ngf3 Bd6 Bg3 O-O Bd3 | The typical London set-up. | arrows=g:f3e5 g:f2f4 b:d3h7

# kid-ideas | opening | أفكار الهندي الملكي | King's Indian ideas
refs: smirin-kid-warfare, bologan-kid, gallagher-kid, bronstein-zurich, flores-structures
rel: pawn-chain, pawn-storm, hypermodern
pz: kingsideAttack
> Black lets White take the centre, then strikes with ...e5 (or ...c5). After d4–d5 the pawn chains point to opposite wings: Black attacks on the kingside with ...Nd7/...Ne8, ...f5–f4, ...g5–g4; White attacks on the queenside with c5, b4 and Nd3.
+ The race: Black must be fast; a well-timed piece sacrifice on the kingside often decides.
+ The "bad" g7-bishop becomes strong if the position opens (after ...f5 or ...c6/...exd4).
line: d4 Nf6 c4 g6 Nc3 Bg7 e4 d6 Nf3 O-O Be2 e5 O-O Nc6 d5 Ne7 Ne1 Nd7 Nd3 f5 | The Mar del Plata: the classic race. | arrows=g:f5f4 g:g6g5 b:c4c5

pz: opening
> 1...c5 creates an unbalanced fight: Black trades the c-pawn for White's d-pawn, gaining a central majority and the half-open c-file, while White gets a lead in development and attacking chances.
+ Black's counterplay: the c-file, ...b5 queenside expansion, the ...Rxc3 exchange sacrifice, the ...d5 break.
+ White's plans: kingside attack (f4–f5, g4–g5, the English Attack) and the d5 outpost.
